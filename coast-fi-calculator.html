How this works

Your FI number is the portfolio that supports your retirement spending: (spending − other income) ÷ safe withdrawal rate, in today's dollars.

Your Coast FI number discounts that back to today at your real (inflation-adjusted) return: FI ÷ (1 + real return)years to retirement. Once your portfolio crosses it, compounding alone carries you to FI by your target age.

The projection compounds annually with contributions credited mid-year, and grows your contribution each year by the increase you set. "Today's $" deflates everything by your inflation assumption so the numbers stay comparable to your current spending.

This is a planning model, not financial advice — real returns arrive in a volatile sequence, not a smooth curve, so treat results as a directional estimate and stress-test with lower return assumptions.
